# Research Interests

I am interested in the working principles of biological and artificial neural network, with a specific interest in how neural systems transform information into useful representations. My central question is how biological brains and artificial neural networks form, organise, and use internal representations and whether comparing the two can reveal principles of neural computation.

I work at the intersection of Computational Neuroscience, NeuroAI, and representation learning. I am particularly interested in when biological and artificial systems develop similar representational structures, what task constraints or learning rules drive those similarities, and when apparently strong brain–model alignment fails to provide a meaningful explanation.

## Biological and Artificial Representations

Biological brains and artificial neural networks differ a lot in their physical implementation, learning mechanisms, and dynamics. Brains are recurrent, stochastic, embodied systems composed of heterogeneous cells, operating across multiple spatial and temporal scales. Artificial networks are usually trained with gradient-based optimisation and are often much simpler in their architecture and dynamics. Yet artificial neural networks can develop representations that predict neural responses or resemble the geometry of neural population activity. I find this convergence weirdly interesting. It raises a central question for me: when does representational similarity reflect a shared computational solution, and when does it arise for more superficial reasons?

I am especially interested in moving beyond the question of whether a model predicts neural data. Instead, I want to ask what properties of the model, its architecture, objective, learning rule, dynamics, or inductive biases, make it align with the brain, and whether these properties can generate testable hypotheses about biological computation.

Ultimately, my central interest is understanding biological neural information processing. I am specifically driven by questions like:

* Why do artificial and biological neural networks often converge to similar representational structures?
* Which properties of the task, environment, architecture, and learning rule determine this convergence?
* Can aligned artificial networks help us infer the actual computational principles of the brain, rather than just predicting its responses?
* How do dynamics, recurrence, oscillations, and population-level interactions shape neural representations over time?
* Can mechanistic analyses of artificial networks give us testable hypotheses for real biological circuits?
* Can we use our understanding of biological networks to build better artificial ones?

My goal is to contribute to a deeper understanding of how neural systems transform sensory input into stable, flexible, and meaningful representations. By studying how dynamics and learning shape these representations, I hope to use computational models to move our understanding of the brain from mere prediction to true explanation.
